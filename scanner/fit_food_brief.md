FIT FOOD SECTION (add to every scan)

Also scan for fit-food news: food and nutrition stories that matter to men who train.
Men's Fitness Online readers lift, run and do HYROX. They want to eat for muscle, fat
loss, performance and health. Australian angle first, then US and UK.

WHAT COUNTS
- New research on protein, creatine, carbs and fuelling, fat loss, muscle, recovery,
  sleep, testosterone, ageing and muscle, gut health and training. Human studies only.
- Supplements: new evidence, regulator action (TGA, FSANZ, FDA, ACCC), recalls,
  contamination, banned substances, label tests.
- GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro) and food: eating on them, muscle loss, new rules.
- Food trends with a gym angle: high-protein products, viral meals (cottage cheese, protein
  coffee), new protein foods in Coles/Woolworths or US stores, price changes on gym staples
  (eggs, chicken, whey).
- What athletes and fit public figures actually eat, when it's in the news now (a new
  interview, a record, a comeback), not old diet listicles.
- Food safety stories that hit gym staples (egg, chicken, protein powder recalls).

LEAVE OUT
- Alcohol, restaurants, general recipes, cooking shows, baking.
- Weight-loss stories aimed at women, detoxes, "superfood" fluff, celebrity diets with no
  training angle.
- Press releases dressed as news, brand launches with no news in them, sponsored content.
- Mouse, cell and test-tube studies (mention one only if it's going viral, and say so).
- Anything older than 7 days, unless a big outlet only just picked it up (then say when it
  first came out).

CHECKS BEFORE YOU LIST A STORY
1. Find the real date. A fresh Google News date can be an old story re-run (ScienceDaily
   often re-runs). Give the date it first came out.
2. Link the original source: the journal paper, the regulator notice, the company
   statement. Not just a news write-up.
3. For a study: journal, year, study type (trial, review, observational), how many people,
   who (age, trained or not), how long, main result in numbers, and who paid for it.
4. Say only what the source says. Don't add causes, motives or words like "black market",
   "deadly" or "banned" unless the source uses them. If a detail came from a photo credit
   or a headline only, say so.
5. If you can't open the source, say "not opened" rather than guessing.

FOR EACH PICK, GIVE
- Headline idea (plain, punchy, no clickbait).
- One line: why a man who trains should care.
- Date it happened + original source link + one other outlet if there is one.
- The study details or regulator facts from check 3.
- The search term people would use, and a rough idea of demand if you can check it.
- Format: news post, explainer, or "add to an existing MFO post". Existing posts worth
  linking or updating include mensfitnessonline.com.au/high-protein-foods/,
  /high-protein-meals/, /high-protein-snacks-that-actually-fill-you-up/, /wpc-vs-wpi/,
  /does-creatine-help-build-muscle/, /does-creatine-cause-hair-loss/,
  /what-supplements-do-you-actually-need/, /glp-1-muscle-loss/, /ozempic-blindness-warning/,
  /are-peptides-safe/, /is-full-fat-dairy-bad-for-you/.
- A score out of 10 for how good a fit it is for MFO, and one line on any risk (thin
  evidence, industry-funded, health claim needing care).

LIMITS
- At most 3 fit-food picks per scan, best first. Quality over quantity.
- If nothing is good enough, say "No fit-food picks today". That's a fine answer.
- Never write the article. Just the picks.
